## Declared policies and measurement algorithm

Fastest approval chooses the fastest usable unlocked model, no planning, approves even blocked reviews, repairs available incidents and compacts only while actively working below 40 context. This optimizes initial throughput, not final score. Planned conservative selects recommended tier when available, largest current quota among that tier, plans every assignment, approves passed reviews and revises blocked ones. Revise-warning uses the same dispatch selection without planning. Escalate-heavy uses that same dispatch selection without planning, escalating blocked reviews while at least 3 trust remains; otherwise revises. Both providers are eligible except the named single-provider variants.

All policies dispatch greedily into idle unlocked slots, prioritize available incident repairs, skip optional work unless the variant includes it, approve/revise immediately at the next 0.05s tick, and roll back an announced gateway incident. They retain the cache provider's default workflow unless explicitly choosing bypass. Upgrade variants buy eligible affordable upgrades immediately in a fixed listed order. These are heuristics with different objectives, not an optimal policy search. Compaction can fail at zero quota; failed attempts do not count as successful decisions. The single-OpenMind route had one distinct engine error (ledger requires 3 upfront quota), retried on simulation ticks until funded. Other route configurations had no review-action rejections.

Utilization = work/support occupied slot-seconds / integral of unlocked slot capacity. Review decisions are instantaneous under these policies, so this does not measure human review effort. Idle seconds = time with no working/quota-paused/supporting sessions, including setup quota regeneration. Quota troughs are sampled every tick; context trough excludes idle sessions. Engine active seconds may differ slightly from sampled elapsed minus idle because events resolve inside ticks. Real milliseconds are in-process wall time per route; process/tool latency is separate.
